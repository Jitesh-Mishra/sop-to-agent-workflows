# Emergency release notification
source: 40 CFR 355.40 to 355.43 (EPA), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-J/part-355/subpart-C
---
The release of a reportable quantity (RQ) of an EHS or CERCLA hazardous substance within any 24-hour period triggers the emergency release notification requirements.
You do not have to provide emergency release notification for any release that results in exposure to persons solely within the boundaries of your facility.
You must provide the immediate emergency release notification to the community emergency coordinator for the LEPC of any area likely to be affected by the release and to the SERC of any State likely to be affected by the release.
For a release that occurs during transportation or from storage incident to transportation, you may meet the requirements of this subpart by notifying the 911 operator of the immediate notification information.
The immediate notification should be oral and shall include the chemical name, whether the substance is an EHS, an estimate of the quantity released, the time and duration of the release, the medium, known health risks, proper precautions, and the name and telephone number of a contact.
The retrieval of this information should not cause a delay in the notification on the emergency response.
Except for releases that occur during transportation or from storage incident to transportation, you must provide a written follow-up emergency notice as soon as practicable after the release.
In the written follow-up emergency notice, you must update the information required in the immediate notification and include actions taken to respond and contain the release, health risks, and advice regarding medical attention.
The SERC and LEPC may request a specific format for this information.
