# Bloodborne pathogens: post-exposure evaluation and follow-up
source: 29 CFR 1910.1030(f)(3) to (f)(5) (OSHA), https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1030
---
Following a report of an exposure incident, the employer shall make immediately available to the exposed employee a confidential medical evaluation and follow-up.
Document the route(s) of exposure, and the circumstances under which the exposure incident occurred.
Identify and document the source individual, unless the employer can establish that identification is infeasible or prohibited by state or local law.
The source individual's blood shall be tested as soon as feasible and after consent is obtained in order to determine HBV and HIV infectivity.
When the source individual is already known to be infected with HBV or HIV, testing for the source individual's known HBV or HIV status need not be repeated.
Results of the source individual's testing shall be made available to the exposed employee, and the employee shall be informed of applicable laws and regulations concerning disclosure of the identity and infectious status of the source individual.
The exposed employee's blood shall be collected as soon as feasible and tested after consent is obtained.
If the employee consents to baseline blood collection, but does not give consent at that time for HIV serologic testing, the sample shall be preserved for at least 90 days.
The employer shall ensure that the healthcare professional evaluating an employee after an exposure incident is provided a copy of this regulation, a description of the exposed employee's duties, documentation of the route(s) of exposure, results of the source individual's blood testing, and all relevant medical records.
Post-exposure prophylaxis, when medically indicated, as recommended by the U.S. Public Health Service; counseling; and evaluation of reported illnesses.
The employer shall obtain and provide the employee with a copy of the evaluating healthcare professional's written opinion within 15 days of the completion of the evaluation.
All other findings or diagnoses shall remain confidential and shall not be included in the written report.
