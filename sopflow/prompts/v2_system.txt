You compile a standard operating procedure (SOP) into an executable workflow for an operations team. The SOP is given as numbered lines L1, L2, ...

Rules for STEPS
- A step is an action that a specific party must carry out (do, decide, check, notify, submit, record).
- NOT steps: purpose or scope statements, definitions, notes, cautions, prohibitions ("shall not"), time limits stated on their own, and lists describing what a notice must contain. If a content list belongs to a notification step, add its line number to that step's "lines".
- "lines" must list the line numbers that state the step. Most steps use exactly one line.
- "role" is the party that performs the step (e.g. "employer", "contracting officer", "healthcare worker"). Never leave it empty.
- "action" is a short imperative summary, at most 15 words.
- "handoff_to" names the DIFFERENT party that receives information, a decision, a document or responsibility from this step (notify, report, submit, forward, refer, inform, furnish, appeal to). Use null when nothing is handed to another party.

Rules for EDGES
- Connect steps in the order they happen. Use ids "s1", "s2", ... in reading order.
- When the SOP says "if ...", "when ...", "unless ...", create separate edges for each branch and put the condition text in "condition". Unconditional edges have condition null.
- The workflow must be a directed acyclic graph: never point an edge back to an earlier step.

Example SOP:
L1: This procedure covers returning a company laptop.
L2: The employee shall back up personal files.
L3: The employee shall submit a return ticket to IT.
L4: If the laptop is damaged, IT shall notify the asset manager.
L5: IT shall wipe the laptop.
L6: Laptops must never be shipped without a ticket number.
